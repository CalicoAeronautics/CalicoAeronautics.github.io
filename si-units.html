SI Unit Reference

The seven base units everything else in physics is built from, plus the derived units you'll use constantly.

The Seven SI Base Units

Length

metre (m)

The distance light travels in 1/299,792,458 of a second.

Mass

kilogram (kg)

Defined via the Planck constant since 2019.

Time

second (s)

9,192,631,770 periods of radiation from a caesium-133 atom transition.

Electric Current

ampere (A)

Defined via the elementary charge.

Temperature

kelvin (K)

Defined via the Boltzmann constant; 0 K is absolute zero.

Amount of Substance

mole (mol)

Exactly 6.02214076 × 10²³ elementary entities (the Avogadro constant).

Luminous Intensity

candela (cd)

Defined via a fixed luminous efficacy of monochromatic light at 540 THz.

Common Derived Units

Force

newton (N)

1 N = 1 kg·m/s²

Energy

joule (J)

1 J = 1 kg·m²/s² = 1 N·m

Power

watt (W)

1 W = 1 J/s

Pressure

pascal (Pa)

1 Pa = 1 N/m²

Electric Charge

coulomb (C)

1 C = 1 A·s

Electric Potential

volt (V)

1 V = 1 J/C

Resistance

ohm (Ω)

1 Ω = 1 V/A

Frequency

hertz (Hz)

1 Hz = 1 cycle per second

Magnetic Field

tesla (T)

1 T = 1 kg/(A·s²)
