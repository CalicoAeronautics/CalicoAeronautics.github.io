# General Relativity, or Why Gravity Isn't a Force

*12 min read &middot; Physics &middot; Relativity, Einstein, Gravity, Spacetime*

Imagine you're in an elevator with no windows. The cable snaps. For the few seconds before you hit the ground, you float &mdash; weightless, exactly as if gravity had switched off.

That thought experiment is the seed of General Relativity. Einstein's insight was that **free fall and weightlessness are the same thing**, and from that single observation, an entire theory of gravity as geometry unfolds.

## Gravity as curvature

In Newton's picture, gravity is a force that reaches across empty space and pulls objects toward each other. In Einstein's picture, mass and energy bend the fabric of spacetime itself, and objects simply follow the straightest possible path through that curved fabric.

A planet orbiting a star isn't being pulled by anything &mdash; it's traveling in a straight line through space that is curved by the star's mass.

## Why this matters

This isn't just philosophy. General Relativity predicts real, measurable effects:

- Light bending around massive objects (gravitational lensing)
- Clocks running slower near massive bodies (gravitational time dilation)
- Gravitational waves, ripples in spacetime from colliding black holes
