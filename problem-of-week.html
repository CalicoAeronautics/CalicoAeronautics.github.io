Problem of the Week

One good problem, once a week. Try it before you look at the hint.

Problem #7

A projectile is fired from ground level at an angle of 40° above the horizontal with an initial speed of 25 m/s. Ignoring air resistance, find the maximum height reached and the total time of flight. (Use g = 9.8 m/s².)

Split the initial velocity into horizontal and vertical components first. Maximum height only depends on the vertical component - the horizontal component never affects how high it goes.
v₀y = 25 sin(40°) ≈ 16.07 m/s.
Max height h = v₀y² / (2g) ≈ 13.2 m.
Time of flight t = 2v₀y / g ≈ 3.28 s.

Problem #6

A 2 kg block slides down a frictionless 30° incline that is 5 m long. Find its speed at the bottom.

Use energy conservation: the height dropped is 5 sin(30°), and all the lost potential energy becomes kinetic energy (mass cancels out).
Height dropped h = 5 sin(30°) = 2.5 m. mgh = ½mv² → v = √(2gh) = √(2 × 9.8 × 2.5) ≈ 7.0 m/s.
Problem #5

Two resistors, 6Ω and 12Ω, are connected in parallel across a 9V battery. Find the total current drawn from the battery.

Find the equivalent resistance of the parallel combination first, then apply Ohm's law.
1/R_eq = 1/6 + 1/12 = 3/12 → R_eq = 4Ω. Total current I = V/R = 9/4 = 2.25 A.
Problem #4

A wave has a frequency of 500 Hz and travels at 340 m/s through air. Find its wavelength.

Use v = fλ, rearranged to solve for λ.
λ = v/f = 340/500 = 0.68 m.
Problem #3

A car accelerates uniformly from rest to 20 m/s in 8 seconds. How far does it travel in that time?

Average velocity for uniform acceleration from rest is half the final velocity.
v_avg = (0 + 20)/2 = 10 m/s. Distance = v_avg × t = 10 × 8 = 80 m.
Problem #2

What is the escape velocity from a planet with the same mass as Earth but half its radius?

Escape velocity v = √(2GM/r). Halving r changes v by a factor of √2.
Earth's escape velocity is about 11.2 km/s. Halving the radius multiplies it by √2, giving roughly 15.8 km/s.
Problem #1

A 3 kg object experiences a net force of 12 N. What is its acceleration?

Newton's Second Law: F = ma.
a = F/m = 12/3 = 4 m/s².
